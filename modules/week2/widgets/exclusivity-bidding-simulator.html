NHL Rights Bidding Simulator

You're the NHL Commissioner. Canada's broadcast rights are expiring. Make the call.

The Situation

Three companies have submitted bids. You can accept Apple's exclusive offer, or split the package among all three bidders. The headline numbers favor splitting — but is the extra revenue worth the complexity and risk?

Rogers: $450M/yr
DAZN: $400M/yr
Apple TV+: $700M/yr (exclusive) or $300M/yr (streaming only)
Option A: Exclusive Deal
$700M
Apple TV+ gets all Canadian NHL rights — national, regional, streaming, playoffs.
Apple TV+ (all rights) +$700M
Guaranteed Annual Revenue $700M
Why This Works
One partner, one app, one relationship. Fans know where to watch. Apple has deep pockets and won't go bankrupt mid-deal.
Option B: Split Package
$1.15B*
Rogers (regional), DAZN (national), Apple (streaming). More revenue on paper.
Rogers (regional) +$450M
DAZN (national) +$400M
Apple TV+ (streaming) +$300M
Coordination & ops costs ? Managing 3 broadcast partners requires dedicated staff, legal overhead, and constant scheduling coordination. More partners = more meetings, more conflicts, more cost. -$40M
Fan fragmentation impact ? When fans can't figure out which app has tonight's game, some just stop watching. Confusion = lower ratings = lower ad revenue = partners pay less next time. -$60M
Risk-Adjusted Revenue $1.05B
Hidden Risk: Partner Instability
DAZN has struggled financially in other markets. If they exit mid-contract, you lose $400M/yr and scramble to find replacement coverage.

The split looks better on paper ($1.05B vs $700M). But is it worth the risk?
